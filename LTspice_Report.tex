\section{S4 -- Physics Check}
\circuit{s4-circuit.png}
  {LTspice testbench for the model-parameter checks (\textit{UA}, \textit{UB}, \textit{VSAT}).}
  {.dc vg 0 3 0.01}

\onefig{s4.png}{0.8\textwidth}{6cm}
  {$I_D$--$V_G$ overlay: nominal, $UA=UB=0$, and $VSAT\times100$.}

\phys{$UA=UB=0$ $\leftrightarrow$ TCAD \emph{Enormal} (mobility degradation) model}{A large vertical gate field pulls carriers against the Si--SiO$_2$ interface where surface-roughness and phonon scattering reduce the effective mobility, and UA and UB model this reduction; setting them to zero removes the degradation, so $I_D$ keeps growing with $V_{GS}$ and $I_{ON}$ becomes noticeably higher than nominal, while $V_{TH}$ and $I_{OFF}$ stay essentially unchanged.}

\phys{$VSAT\times100$ $\leftrightarrow$ TCAD \emph{HighFieldSaturation} model}{In a short channel the lateral field is high enough for carriers to reach the saturation velocity $v_{sat}$, which caps the current at roughly $W C_{ox}(V_{GS}-V_{TH})v_{sat}$; raising VSAT by 100$\times$ effectively removes this limit, so the drain current follows the mobility-limited (quadratic) law, $I_{ON}$ increases strongly and the output curves no longer saturate early.}

\concl{Velocity saturation limits $I_{ON}$ the most in a 65\,nm device, because the lateral field is very high, with vertical-field mobility degradation as a secondary limit.}

\subsection{Voltage Transfer Characteristic}
\onefig{s7.png}{0.6\textwidth}{5.5cm}
  {$V_{out}$ versus $V_{in}$ ($0\to V_{DD}$) showing $V_{OH}$, $V_{OL}$, $V_M$ and the high-gain region.}


\phys{Device-physics explanation}{For $V_{in}\approx0$ the NMOS is off and the PMOS is on, so the output is pulled to $V_{DD}$ ($V_{OH}$); as $V_{in}$ nears $V_M$ both transistors are in saturation, the output falls sharply and the gain is maximum; for $V_{in}\approx V_{DD}$ the NMOS is on and the PMOS is off, so the output is pulled to ground ($V_{OL}$). The output is always the logical complement of the input, which is why the circuit is called an inverter.}

\concl{The inverter gives full rail-to-rail swing ($V_{OH}=V_{DD}$, $V_{OL}=0$), and the ratio $W_p/W_n=2$ compensates the lower hole mobility so that $V_M$ lies close to $V_{DD}/2$, giving a nearly symmetric transfer curve and balanced noise margins.}
